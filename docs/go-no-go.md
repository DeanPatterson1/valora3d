# Valora3D go / no-go checkpoints

Owner-approved on 2026-09-29. The point: get a clear answer on whether this is a business, spending limited
time and money, and decide in advance when to stop. These rules were set before launch on purpose; don't
renegotiate them at a checkpoint.

## The rule that matters most

**Do not add features to fix a lack of customers.** If people are not paying, the next step is talking to
buyers (price, audience, message), not building.

## Checkpoints

| # | When | Goal | If missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Private launch by **2026-10-20** | A real person signs up, pays, and receives a video, end to end, on production | Fix blockers only. No new features until this passes. |
| 2 | Launch + 30 days | **20 people try it, 5 pay** anything | Change ONE thing (price, target buyer, or photo-to-3D via Tripo), then run another 30 days. |
| 3 | Launch + 60 days | **10+ paying customers**, or clear repeat buyers | **Stop building.** Keep the site live as a portfolio piece, keep costs near zero, move on. |
| - | Any time | Running cost stays under **~$50 / month** until revenue covers it | Pause the paid services (RunPod endpoint to 0 workers, AI key cap). |

Write the launch date here when checkpoint 1 passes: `LAUNCH_DATE = ____`.

## Focus

During the 60-day test, ModVision and the separate rendering business stay in maintenance mode. That way
the test gives a clear answer on one thing, instead of slow progress on three.

## What counts

- **Try:** created an account and made at least one preview with their own model.
- **Pay:** any completed Paddle payment (the $2 first clip counts), excluding the owner, friends, and refunds.
- Numbers come from the admin console / growth dashboard, not from memory.

## Inputs behind these numbers (2026-09-29)

- Blind buyer panel (two independent reviewers, 10 sample clips): quality about 6.5-7 / 10. The rain car
  scenes scored 8; the studio product shots 6-8 and felt samey; the current homepage hero scored 4-5.
  Buyers would pay $3-15 per clip depending on who they are. Online sellers and agencies are the best fit;
  hobbyists and dealers usually have no 3D model file.
- Competitor scan: no tool turns a customer's own 3D model into a video. Generic AI video costs under ~$4
  per clip but can't show the exact product; freelancers charge $50-350.
- Biggest risks: buyers need a .glb, and the shots feel samey. Photo-to-3D (e.g. Tripo) and more scene and
  camera variety are the two levers, and each can be tried only at checkpoint 2, not before launch.
